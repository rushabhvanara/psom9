Power System Stability Improvement Using FACTS Devices

Simulation-based study of an uncompensated transmission system, TCSC, SSSC and UPFC
1. Learning Objective
This virtual laboratory demonstrates how Flexible AC Transmission System (FACTS) controllers improve transient stability and power-transfer capability of a transmission system. A three-phase disturbance is simulated and the resulting rotor-angle response, electrical power transfer and stability margin are compared for four configurations.
Student task: Run the disturbance for each case and observe the change in maximum rotor angle, settling time, power transfer and stability margin.

10. Interpretation for Students

Uncompensated: The transmission line has its original reactance. During a severe disturbance, the accelerating power may become large and the rotor angle can increase significantly.

TCSC: The Thyristor Controlled Series Capacitor changes the effective series reactance. Reducing the effective reactance increases the maximum transferable electrical power according to the power-angle equation.

SSSC: The Static Synchronous Series Compensator injects a controllable series voltage. In this simplified model its stabilizing effect is represented as an effective reduction of transmission reactance together with dynamic damping.

UPFC: The Unified Power Flow Controller combines series and shunt converter functions. It provides simultaneous control of voltage magnitude, transmission impedance and phase angle, giving the strongest stabilization in this educational model.

Important: This simulation is an educational reduced-order model. A detailed electromagnetic-transient study would require a full network model, generator excitation/governor models, converter controls, protection and switching dynamics.

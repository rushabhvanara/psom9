Theory · Mathematical model

Transient Stability Model

M′ d2 dt2 δ = Pm − Pe
2 H d2 dt2 δ = π f0 ( Pm − Pe )

During the fault, electrical power transfer decreases while mechanical input remains approximately constant. The rotor therefore accelerates. Faster restoration of electrical transfer reduces the accumulated rotor kinetic energy and improves the transient-stability margin.

FACTS comparison

Controller Performance

Device Primary action Equivalent effect in this model Expected stability effect
SVC Shunt reactive compensation Voltage-support increase Improves voltage profile and transfer capability
STATCOM Voltage-source shunt compensation Strong dynamic voltage support Rapid voltage recovery during disturbances
TCSC Series compensation Reduces effective X Increases Pmax
UPFC Combined series + shunt control Simultaneous voltage and power-flow control Highest flexibility in this simplified model
Interpretation: A FACTS controller does not “remove” the disturbance. It changes the electrical characteristics of the network so that electrical power can be restored more effectively and rotor acceleration is reduced.
Laboratory procedure

Suggested Experiment

  1. Select No FACTS device and run the simulation.
  2. Record maximum rotor angle, clearing time and stability margin.
  3. Select SVC, STATCOM, TCSC and UPFC one at a time.
  4. Keep all other parameters unchanged.
  5. Compare the rotor-angle trajectories and power-transfer curves.
  6. Increase fault-clearing time and determine the approximate critical clearing time.
  7. Repeat for different FACTS control strengths.
Stability margin = tcr tclear − 1

Positive margin indicates that the selected clearing time is below the estimated critical clearing time in this educational numerical model.
